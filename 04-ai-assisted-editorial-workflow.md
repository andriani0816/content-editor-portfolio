# AI-Assisted Editorial Workflow: From Research to Publication-Ready Content

**Portfolio type:** Independent Content Portfolio Project

**Content type:** Editorial workflow and AI quality case study

**Primary skills:** AI-assisted editing, fact-checking, content quality assurance, and editorial judgment

**Objective:** Demonstrate responsible use of AI within a human-controlled editorial workflow

## Project Objective

This project demonstrates how AI can support editorial work without replacing human judgment. The workflow combines AI-assisted research and drafting with source verification, fact-checking, content editing, quality review, and final human approval.

The objective is not simply to produce content faster, but to use AI selectively where it adds value while maintaining accuracy, usefulness, consistency, and reader trust.

## Editorial Scenario

A content editor is preparing a consumer-facing article about gaming, technology, or a digital marketplace. AI tools may help accelerate research, organise information, suggest structures, or produce an initial draft. However, AI-generated material cannot be assumed to be accurate, current, relevant, or publication-ready.

The editor therefore remains responsible for validating information, improving the content, and deciding what should ultimately be published.

## Workflow

### 1. Define the Brief and Search Intent

Before using AI, clarify the article's purpose, target audience, search intent, key questions, and expected reader action. This prevents the tool from determining the editorial direction by default.

### 2. Research and Source Validation

Gather information from credible and relevant sources. Check important claims against original or authoritative sources where possible, especially product details, prices, availability, policies, dates, and other information that may change.

### 3. AI-Assisted Ideation and Drafting

AI can be used to suggest outlines, identify questions a reader may have, reorganise notes, or create an initial draft. Its output is treated as working material rather than finished copy.

### 4. Human Fact-Checking

Verify factual claims before publication. Remove unsupported statements, correct inaccuracies, distinguish facts from assumptions, and avoid presenting uncertain information as established fact.

### 5. Editorial Review

Edit the draft for clarity, structure, relevance, tone, readability, and usefulness. Remove repetition and generic language. Make sure headings accurately represent the sections beneath them and that the article answers the reader's main question efficiently.

### 6. SEO and Commercial Quality Review

Review the title, headings, search intent, keyword use, internal-link opportunities, and calls to action. SEO improvements should support the reader rather than create unnatural or repetitive copy. Commercial content should help readers make informed decisions without overstating benefits.

### 7. Final Human Quality Assurance

Before publication, perform a final check for factual accuracy, source quality, grammar, formatting, consistency, broken or inappropriate links, unsupported claims, and potentially outdated information. Publication remains a human decision.

## Example Quality-Control Checklist

- Does the content answer the user's likely search intent?
- Are important factual claims supported by reliable sources?
- Have time-sensitive details been checked for currency?
- Has AI-generated information been independently reviewed?
- Is the article clear, concise, and logically structured?
- Are repetitive, vague, or generic passages removed?
- Are SEO elements useful rather than forced?
- Are commercial claims balanced and supportable?
- Would a reader understand what to do or consider next?
- Has a human completed the final publication review?

## Key Editorial Risks and Controls

**Hallucinated or unsupported information:** Verify claims against credible sources and remove information that cannot be substantiated.

**Outdated information:** Recheck details that can change, including pricing, availability, product features, and marketplace policies.

**Generic AI language:** Rewrite for specificity, usefulness, natural tone, and the intended audience.

**Over-optimisation for search:** Prioritise reader value and natural language while maintaining clear search relevance.

**Loss of editorial accountability:** Keep a human editor responsible for verification, revision, and the final publication decision.

## Case Study Takeaway

AI can improve editorial efficiency, but speed alone does not create high-quality content. A responsible workflow combines AI assistance with research, verification, critical editing, and human accountability.

This case study demonstrates an editorial approach in which AI supports the process while the editor retains control over accuracy, quality, usefulness, and publication readiness.
